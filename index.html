01 — The gap

Watching money happen is not the same as understanding it.

Teenagers in Kenya grow up surrounded by financial decisions — borrowing, saving, spending, planning. But almost none of it is explained in a way that connects to a 15-year-old’s actual life.

The concepts live in textbooks and in adult conversations. The understanding rarely makes it across. That gap is what this project is about.

1

We see the transaction, not the thinking

A parent sends M-Pesa. A cousin borrows. An aunt joins a chama. The decisions are visible — the reasoning behind them is not.

2

The words don’t match the life

Budgeting, interest, collateral — taught in the abstract, and applied nowhere near a matatu fare or a 50-bob data bundle.

3

Confidence arrives too late

By the time money starts to feel legible, the first real decisions have usually already been made — sometimes with a fee attached.

02 — What we’re exploring

Six things nobody explained properly.

These came up again and again in our conversations. They are not chapters from a textbook — they are the questions people our age actually ask.

01

M-Pesa isn’t a wallet

It’s a door. Understanding where money actually goes after the green message is the first unlock — and the one most people skip.

02

The interest you don’t see

Fuliza, loan apps and “small” borrowing. What really happens when you repay late, and why the fee is charged before you notice it.

03

Chamas & savings groups

The most common financial institution in Kenya, and the least explained to young people. Who holds the money, and who decides?

04

Black tax & family money

The unwritten rules about who you support, when, and how much. Nobody signs a contract, but everybody feels it.

05

The hustle economy

Irregular income, side gigs and term-time work. Why “budget monthly” doesn’t always fit a life that pays weekly.

06

Betting & the big win

Why the odds feel different than they are, and how a near-miss is designed to feel like a signal.

03 — Money myths

Four things we believed. Tap a card.

We did too. That’s the point — these aren’t stupid ideas, they’re just ideas nobody corrected.

Myth “Saving means having money left over at the end of the month.” Tap to reveal
Reality Saving is a decision you make before spending, not after. KES 20 moved on purpose is a system. KES 2,000 left at month-end is luck. Tap to flip back
Myth “Borrowing is free as long as I repay on time.” Tap to reveal
Reality The fee is charged when you borrow, not when you’re late. Read the number before you tap — not after the message arrives. Tap to flip back
Myth “Talking about money is rude.” Tap to reveal
Reality Silence is how bad money habits get passed down. Naming a price, a fee or a limit is normal. It is not shameful. Tap to flip back
Myth “A budget means you can’t enjoy anything.” Tap to reveal
Reality A budget is a plan for what you’ve decided matters. It protects the fun — it doesn’t remove it. Tap to flip back
04 — What this is

A website, not an app. On purpose.

There are already plenty of tools that will calculate things for you. What’s missing is the thinking underneath the tool.

What this is

  • Plain-language explanations of money concepts
  • Real Kenyan context — M-Pesa, chamas, matatu fares
  • Questions you can actually ask an adult
  • Written and reviewed by people your age

What this isn’t

  • A banking app
  • A savings or loan calculator
  • Financial advice
  • A get-rich-quick plan
