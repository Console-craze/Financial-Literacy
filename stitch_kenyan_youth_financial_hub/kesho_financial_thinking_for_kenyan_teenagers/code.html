01 — THE GAP

Watching money happen is not the same as understanding it.

In Kenya, a fifteen-year-old sends money on their mother's phone, calculates fares in fluctuating traffic, buys data by the day, and overhears debates about hospital bills and chama contributions.

Yet the school syllabus discusses cheques, balance sheets, and bank ledger accounts that few families use. Between the adult financial world that is everywhere and the formal education meant to explain it, there is silence.

“We don't lack access to transactions. We lack anyone explaining the consequences before they arrive.”

88% Handle digital money weekly
11% Taught what loan interest means
1

We see the transaction, not the thinking

We know the PIN, the green confirmation prompt, and the agent fees. But nobody sees why a parent took a loan, why they saved in a chama instead of a bank, or why money runs short mid-month.

2

The words don’t match the life

Textbooks define "Compound Interest" with formulas, but not Fuliza daily rollover fees. They teach "Commercial Banks" while everyday life happens on mobile networks and communal table-banking.

3

Confidence arrives too late

Most people in Kenya learn about debt traps, guarantor liability, and credit bureaus through penalties, defaulted group contributions, and locked phones. We think 15 is the time to start.

03 — MONEY MYTHS

Four things we believed. Tap a card.

Click any card to flip the myth and reveal the architectural reality behind it.

MYTH #1

“Saving means having money left over at the end of the month.”

Click to reveal reality touch_app
REALITY #1

Saving is a decision made before spending begins, not what survives the end of the cycle. Money with no predetermined job will always find an emergency or impulse to spend itself on.

Principle: Pay yourself first
MYTH #2

“Borrowing is free as long as I repay on time.”

Click to reveal reality touch_app
REALITY #2

Access fees, rollover administration levies, and transaction tariffs trigger the moment funds leave the server. Instant liquidity is never free; it is simply billed quietly in small fractions.

Principle: Total cost of capital
MYTH #3

“Talking about money with family is rude.”

Click to reveal reality touch_app
REALITY #3

Silence is how financial anxiety, blind assumptions, and systemic bad habits get passed down across generations. Transparent family dialogue is protection, not disrespect.

Principle: Household transparency
MYTH #4

“A budget means you can’t enjoy anything.”

Click to reveal reality touch_app
REALITY #4

A budget protects your fun without guilt. When you assign 200 shillings to entertainment ahead of time, spending it is an intentional plan, not a secret regret.

Principle: Guilt-free allocation

04 — CLARITY OF PURPOSE

A website, not an app. On purpose.

We didn't build software to hold your money. We built reading material to strengthen your judgment before anyone asks for it.

What this is

  • check
    Plain-language explanations Written without bankers' jargon, academic abstractions, or patronizing moral lessons.
  • check
    Real Kenyan context Centered on M-Pesa statements, daily chama rosters, matatu fluctuations, and boda-boda leases.
  • check
    Questions you can ask adults Concrete scripts and questions to start respectful conversations at home without sounding confrontational.
  • check
    Student written & reviewed Researched, drafted, and tested by teenagers across secondary schools before publication.

What this isn't

  • close
    A banking app or wallet We never collect credentials, connect phone numbers, or move funds between accounts.
  • close
    A savings or loan calculator Calculators give formulas; our aim is contextual clarity and behavioural intuition.
  • close
    Formal financial advice We are students documenting lived experiences, not certified financial intermediaries or brokers.
  • close
    A get-rich-quick plan No crypto drops, forex trading signals, or artificial promises of overnight wealth.
